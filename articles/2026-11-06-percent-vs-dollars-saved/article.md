A sale banner usually shows one number: "up to 80% off". It is a true statement if one game in the sale has that discount. It says nothing about the other games, and nothing about what you pay.

Percent, dollars saved and the price you pay are three different numbers. They rank the same games in three different orders. This guide shows that on five real discounts and gives a short check to run before you click buy.

## The short version

- **"Up to X%" is the best case in the sale, not the typical one.** Look at the game you want, not at the banner.
- **A bigger percent does not always save more money.** A 90% cut on an old $29.99 game saves less than a 90% cut on a $39.99 game.
- **The percent is measured against a "was" price.** If that price is not one the game really sold at, the percent means little.
- **The number to compare is the price you will pay,** next to the price history of the same game.

## What "up to" really claims

"Up to 80% off" promises that at least one item is cut by 80%. The others can be at 10%. Steam's own rules for publishers show how wide that range is. The Steamworks discount documentation says, under "Discount Thresholds & Durations": "It's not possible to discount a product by less than 10% or more than 95%." So on Steam a single sale can contain items from 10% to 95% off, and the headline will quote the top.

The same page says you can pick a different percentage for each package ("You might not want to match the discount on every package."). In other words, a publisher sale is a set of separate discounts under one banner.

## Five discounts, three rankings

On November 6, 2026 our price check read these GOG discounts. Dollars saved is the "was" price minus the sale price, calculated from our data.

| Game | Was | Now | Percent | Dollars saved |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shadow Tactics: Blades of the Shogun | $39.99 | $3.99 | -90% | $36.00 |
| Metro Exodus | $29.99 | $2.99 | -90% | $27.00 |
| Darkest Dungeon | $24.99 | $3.75 | -85% | $21.24 |
| This War of Mine | $19.99 | $2.99 | -85% | $17.00 |
| SimCity 4 Deluxe Edition | $19.99 | $4.99 | -75% | $15.00 |

Rank them by percent and SimCity 4 comes last. Rank them by the price you pay and Metro Exodus and This War of Mine tie for cheapest at $2.99, and SimCity 4 is again last at $4.99. Rank them by dollars saved and the order changes again: Darkest Dungeon saves more than This War of Mine even though both are -85%.

None of this makes one game a better buy. A 75% discount on a game you will play is worth more than a 90% discount on one you will not. The point is that the percent alone cannot decide it.

## Where the percent comes from

The percent is always measured against a reference price. For Steam, the Steamworks documentation says it aims to post discounts "in reference to a base price that has been in place for at least 30 days" (section "Discounting Rules"). That is the store's stated aim for its own platform. It does not tell you what any other store or any key seller does.

In the United States, the Federal Trade Commission's guide on former price comparisons (16 CFR 233.1) says a former price is a fair basis for comparison when it is "the actual, bona fide price at which the article was offered to the public on a regular basis". It calls a former price that is "not bona fide but fictitious" a false bargain. The guide is aimed at advertisers in the US, and rules in other countries differ. Our guide on [telling a real discount from a fake one](/guides/real-vs-fake-game-discounts/) goes through that check in more detail.

## A five-minute check

1. **Find the price you would pay,** not the percent. Write it down.
2. **Subtract it from the "was" price** to get the dollars saved. If the number is small, a big percent was never a big deal.
3. **Open the price history** of that game on a tracker, or read our guide on [how to read a game's price history](/guides/how-to-read-game-price-history/). Has it sold at this price before?
4. **Ask whether you want the game at this price,** with no sale. If not, the discount is not the reason to buy.
5. **Check the end date.** A sale that ends in ten days leaves time to decide. A countdown does not need you to hurry.

## What we did not check

We read only five GOG discounts on one day, so they are examples, not a sample of all sales. The Humble publisher sale page we wanted to use as an example is on a site that is closed to our tools, and the news page that covered it did not open either, so we do not quote any of its numbers. The Steam publisher page we tried loaded without any products. We did not check how other stores set their "was" price, and we did not read consumer rules outside the United States. Prices and sales change, and the table above is only true for November 6, 2026.
