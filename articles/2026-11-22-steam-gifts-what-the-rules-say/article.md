Gift season is close, and a game is an easy present. It is also a present with unusual rules. Once the person accepts it, the game lives on their account, and it cannot move anywhere else.

This guide covers only what two Valve pages say: the Steam refunds page and the Steam Subscriber Agreement. Valve's support pages did not open for our tools, so some gift details are left out on purpose. See "What we did not check".

## The short version

- **An unredeemed gift can be refunded** "within the standard 14-day/two-hour refund period", according to the refunds page.
- **A redeemed gift can be refunded only if the recipient starts the request.** The buyer cannot do it for them.
- **The money goes back to the buyer,** not to the person who got the gift.
- **A game cannot be passed on after it is redeemed.** The agreement says "Subscriptions are not transferable" (section 9.B).

## What the refunds page says about gifts

The page has a short passage on gifts. It says: "Unredeemed gifts may be refunded within the standard 14-day/two-hour refund period." In plain words, the clock that applies to a game you buy for yourself also applies to a gift that nobody has accepted yet. The 14 days count from the purchase.

For a gift that has been redeemed, the page says: "Redeemed gifts may be refunded under the same conditions if the gift recipient initiates the refund." So the two-hour playtime limit counts the recipient's playing, and the recipient is the one who has to ask. A buyer who regrets the present a week later cannot reach into someone else's library.

The page also says: "Funds used to purchase the gift will be returned to the original purchaser." That is useful to know in two situations. The person you gave the game to may already own it, or may simply not want it. If they refund it, you get the money, not them. Our guide to [how Steam refunds work](/guides/how-steam-refunds-work/) explains the 14-day, 2-hour rule itself.

## What the agreement says about moving a game

The Steam Subscriber Agreement does not use the word gift in the passages we could read. What it does say is about transfers in general:

- Section 9.B: "Subscriptions are not transferable."
- Section 3.D: "Subscriptions acquired in any Subscription Marketplace are license rights." Valve does not recognize transfers made outside Steam.
- Section 1.C: your account is strictly personal, and you may not sell or charge others for the right to use it.

Put together, a Steam gift works like this: the game is delivered once, to one account. After that, the recipient cannot hand it to a third person, and the buyer cannot take it back. If you want a present that can be passed along, that is not what a Steam game is. Our guide on [Steam keys and DRM-free games](/guides/steam-key-vs-drm-free/) shows how a DRM-free store describes the difference.

## Who pays when someone else orders

Section 3.B makes the account holder responsible for all charges, and says that includes orders made by anyone who uses the account. If a child, a friend or a relative buys a gift on your account, the bill is yours. This is one more reason to keep an account private.

## Before you buy a gift: a short checklist

1. **Does the person already own the game?** If they do, the gift may end in a refund to you, not in a present.
2. **Will they open it in time?** An unredeemed gift is refundable only inside the 14-day window.
3. **Is it for the right country?** Prices differ by country. See [why a PC game costs a different amount in each country](/guides/why-game-prices-differ-by-country/). We did not check whether Steam restricts gifts across regions.
4. **Would a different present be safer?** If you are unsure of their taste, ask for their wishlist first.

## What we did not check

Valve's support pages (help.steampowered.com) did not load for our tools, and the Subscriber Agreement text we could read does not mention gifts directly. So we did not verify: how long a gift stays open before it expires, whether a recipient can decline it, whether gifts are limited between regions or currencies, or how gifting works for a game bought as a key elsewhere. We did not test a real gift refund. Consumer law differs by country.
