Most people buy a game the day they want it, and most games are cheaper a few weeks later. You do not have to guess when. Steam publishes its sale dates for developers months in advance, and a wishlist can tell you the moment a game you care about goes on sale.

Waiting is not always smart, though. This guide shows what the official pages say about sale dates and wishlist alerts, and gives you a short way to decide.

## The short version

- **Steam runs four seasonal sales a year**, and the dates are published ahead. The next one listed is the Winter Sale, December 17, 2026 to January 4, 2027.
- **A wishlist alerts you** when a game goes on a discount of 20% or more that lasts over 8 hours. Smaller or shorter discounts do not trigger the alert.
- **A scheduled sale is not a promise.** The publisher decides whether a game takes part. Wait only for games you are in no hurry to play.

## What Steam says about its sale calendar

The Steamworks page "Upcoming Events" is written for developers, but it is public and it lists the dates. On the page, in the section "Seasonal Sales":

> "Four times a year, we run Steam-wide seasonal sale events."

When we read it, three were scheduled: the Winter Sale (December 17, 2026 to January 4, 2027), the Spring Sale (March 18 to 25, 2027) and the Summer Sale (June 24 to July 8, 2027). The Autumn Sale 2027 was marked as not yet scheduled.

The same page lists smaller themed events, which it calls Fests. On that date the list included Cooking Fest (October 12 to 19, 2026), Steam Scream V (October 26 to November 2, 2026) and Auto-Battler RPG Fest (November 16 to 23, 2026). The page says that "discount games are prioritized" at these events, so some games do get cheaper, but only those that fit the theme and whose publishers join in.

Do not confuse these with Steam Next Fest. The page describes it as a celebration where "upcoming (unreleased) games with a playable demo are eligible to participate". It is about demos, not about discounts. Its next run was listed as October 19 to 26, 2026.

## Who can be in a seasonal sale

The same page says about the seasonal sales: "Any game released at least 30 days before the event start date is welcome to participate with a discount." Note the words "is welcome". Taking part is a choice, and it is made by the publisher.

The Steamworks page on discounting adds a detail. Seasonal sales "do not have 30-day discount cooldowns", and they are exempt from the cooldowns of other discounts. But a product on a 30-day release cooldown or a 30-day price increase cooldown cannot join a seasonal sale. In plain words:

- A game that came out less than 30 days before a sale will not be in it. Do not wait for a discount on a brand-new release.
- A game whose price was just raised can miss the sale too.
- A game that had a small discount last week can still be in the big sale.

## What a wishlist does for you

The Steamworks "Wishlist" page says that when a wishlisted game "releases or goes on discount, those players automatically receive a notification". It also gives the conditions for the sale alert:

- The discount must be "at or above 20%".
- It must last "over 8 hours".
- Email notifications are "limited to 2 weeks" in frequency for each game, and you only get email if you have "verified" your email address with Steam.

What this means for you: add the games you want, verify your email, and let Steam do the watching. A 15% discount will not ping you, and a flash discount shorter than 8 hours will not either. If you want to see small drops too, a price tracker or a look at the store page is the way. Our guide on [real and fake discounts](/guides/real-vs-fake-game-discounts/) explains how to check what a "was" price means.

The wishlist page describes the alert system on the developer side. We did not check the exact wording of the settings in your Steam account, so look at the notification options there.

## When to wait and when to buy

1. **Is it a new release?** Under 30 days old, it cannot be in a seasonal sale. Waiting for a seasonal discount makes little sense yet.
2. **Do you need it now?** If friends play it this weekend, a few dollars saved may not be worth missing the fun.
3. **Is a sale close?** Check the dates above. If the Winter Sale is two weeks away and the game is not urgent, waiting costs you little.
4. **Is the game on your wishlist?** If not, add it. The alert is free.
5. **Is the discount deep enough for you?** Decide your price in advance. A sale at a price you would not pay is not a reason to buy.

## What we did not check

- We could not open the support page for Steam's wishlist settings, so we do not describe how to switch alerts on or off in your account.
- We did not check the sale calendars of other stores. Their sale dates are not as widely published, and GOG's support pages did not open for us.
- The dates come from a page for developers and can be moved. The Autumn Sale 2027 had no date when we read it.
- We cannot tell you which games will be in a given sale, or at what price. Publishers decide that.
