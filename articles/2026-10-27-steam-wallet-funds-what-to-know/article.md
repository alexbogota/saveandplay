Adding money to a Steam Wallet feels like putting cash in a pocket. During a sale it can even look smart: top up once, then buy several games fast. But Valve's pages describe the wallet as something quite different from a bank balance, and a few of its rules are easy to miss until you need them.

This guide sticks to what two Valve pages say: the Steam Subscriber Agreement and the Steam refunds page. It does not tell you whether you should use the wallet.

## The short version

- **Wallet funds are prepaid credit, not money.** The agreement says they "have no cash value and are not exchangeable for cash."
- **You can get a top-up back only for 14 days, and only if you have not spent any of it.**
- **Funds stay with your account.** They are "non-refundable and non-transferable" in the agreement, apart from the refund rule above.
- **If Valve ends your account for breaking the rules, unused funds are not refunded.**

## What the wallet is

Section 3.C of the Steam Subscriber Agreement says: "The Steam Wallet is neither a bank account nor any kind of payment instrument." It is a prepaid balance for ordering content and services on Steam.

You can add funds by credit card, prepaid card, promotional code or another payment method Steam accepts. The agreement says Valve sets the maximum balance and may change it. It also sets a limit: over any 24-hour period, the balance plus spending may not go above "US$2,000 or its equivalent in your applicable local currency." Valve must email you 60 days before it changes the limits.

## Getting money back

The refunds page has a separate rule for wallet money. You can "request a refund for Steam Wallet funds within fourteen days of purchase", and only "if you have not used any of those funds."

So the common case is simple: top up $20, change your mind the same week, spend nothing, ask for a refund. If you spend even part of the top-up, this route is closed for that purchase.

The refunds page also says where the money goes: "in Steam Wallet funds or through the same payment method you used to make the purchase." If the original method cannot take a refund, the wallet is credited the full amount.

The 14-day, 2-hour rule for games is a different rule. Our guide to [how Steam refunds work](/guides/how-steam-refunds-work/) covers it. One link between the two: a game you bought with wallet funds can still be refunded under the game rules.

## What you cannot do

- **Cash out.** Funds have "no cash value" and "have no value outside Steam."
- **Move funds to someone else.** Wallet funds are non-transferable, and section 1.C says you may not sell or charge others for the right to use your account.
- **Use a different country's price.** Section 3.A prohibits using IP proxying or similar methods "to order or purchase at pricing not applicable to your geography." Valve may end your account access for it. See also [why game prices differ by country](/guides/why-game-prices-differ-by-country/).

## When unused funds are lost

Two cases in the agreement matter. If Valve terminates your account for a violation or for improper or illegal activity, section 9.C says "no refund, including of any Subscription fees or of any unused funds in your Steam Wallet, will be granted." And if Valve changes the wallet limits and you reject them, section 3.C says Valve has no obligation to refund the credits left in your wallet.

The agreement also mentions an expiry rule that applies only to Japanese subscribers: funds unused for six months after being added expire, as Japanese law requires. Rules for unclaimed funds and consumer rights differ by country, so check your own. This is not legal advice.

## Gifts

The refunds page says unredeemed gifts may be refunded within the standard 14-day, 2-hour period. A redeemed gift can be refunded if the recipient starts the request, and the money goes back to the person who bought it.

## Before you top up: a short checklist

1. **Do you know what you will buy?** If not, buying a specific game directly leaves you with a game, not a balance.
2. **Is the amount small enough to lose track of?** A balance can sit on an account for years.
3. **Do you plan to spend within 14 days?** If not, remember that after that window the refund route is gone.
4. **Is your account safe?** The balance is tied to it. Keep the login private; section 1.C says the account is strictly personal.
5. **Are you buying from your own country?** Do not use tricks to get another region's price.
