A game page often has a second list under the main button: the DLC, short for downloadable content. Some of it is a free soundtrack. Some of it costs real money. Some is a big expansion that changes what the game is.

When a headline says "free expansion" or "free DLC", it is easy to miss what you still need to own, and what happens if you pay for the paid kind and change your mind. This guide shows what the store pages say and a quick check you can do before you click.

## The short version

- **Free and paid DLC sit in the same list.** Read the price on each line, not just the first one.
- **DLC bought in the Steam store can be refunded within fourteen days** if it has not been consumed, modified or transferred. Some third-party DLC is marked nonrefundable before you buy.
- **In-game purchases are a different rule.** In games not made by Valve, they depend on the developer.
- **A headline about free DLC does not replace the store page.** Check the date, the price line and what the page says you need.

## Free DLC and paid DLC look alike

On the GOG page for Dead Age 2, read on November 28, 2026, the DLC list shows the Dead Age 2 Original Soundtrack at "0", free. The game itself was listed as "Now on giveaway", with "Offer ends on: 11/30/2026 16:00 EET". That is a limited offer on the game, and the free soundtrack is a separate line.

On the Steam page for Hollow Knight: Silksong, the "Content For This Game" block lists one item, the Official Soundtrack, at $11.99. The same page has an "Add all DLC to Cart" button. So a button that says "all" can include something that costs money.

Valve's own developer documentation says in its overview that "Steam supports both free and paid downloadable content (DLC)". It adds that DLC "can be registered via CD key or purchased from the Steam store." For you, this means a free line is not a different kind of thing. It is the same list, with a price of zero.

We did not find a Sea of Sorrow expansion on the Silksong Steam page when we read it, so we say nothing here about its price, date or requirements. Wait until the page itself shows them.

## DLC refunds on Steam

Steam's refund page has a separate line for DLC. It says: "DLC purchased from the Steam store is refundable within fourteen days of purchase", so long as the DLC "has not been consumed, modified or transferred".

It also says some third-party DLC can be nonrefundable, and that "These exceptions will be clearly marked as nonrefundable on the Store page prior to purchase." So the time to look for that mark is before you pay.

In-game purchases follow another rule. For Valve's own games the page gives forty-eight hours. For other games it says "Third-party developers will have the option to enable refunds for in-game items on these terms", and otherwise in-game purchases in non-Valve games "are not refundable through Steam." Our guide to [how Steam refunds work](/guides/how-steam-refunds-work/) covers the main game rule.

## How to check a DLC line in five minutes

1. **Scroll to the DLC list** on the game page (on Steam, "Content For This Game"). Write down each line's price.
2. **Look for a nonrefundable mark** next to any paid item before you pay.
3. **Check what you already own.** Valve's documentation says a game "will need to check whether or not a user owns the DLC", and that its Properties, DLC tab shows what you own: "If the DLC does not appear there, you do not own it."
4. **Read the date on any "free" offer.** On GOG the end time was printed on the page, with a zone label. Our guide on [sale end times](/guides/sale-end-times-time-zones/) shows how to convert it.
5. **Re-open the page just before you click.** News sites can lag behind the store.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Is the line free, or only the first item on the page?
- Do I need the base game to use it? Valve's page does not say, so read the store page for that game.
- If I pay, is it DLC from the store, or an in-game purchase with other refund rules?
- Would I still want it if it cost its full price?

## What we did not check

- We did not find any Valve page that states whether a game must be owned before its DLC can be used. The developer documentation says DLC depots are part of the base app, but does not address a buyer who lacks the game. Read the individual page.
- We did not read GOG's own DLC or refund pages for this guide, so we say nothing about GOG refunds for DLC.
- We read two game pages, not every store. We did not check Epic or key resellers.
- The Sea of Sorrow expansion for Hollow Knight: Silksong was not on the Steam page we read, so we did not check it.
