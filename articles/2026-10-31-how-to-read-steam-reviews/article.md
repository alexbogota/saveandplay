The review label under a game's title feels like a verdict. It is a summary, and it is built by rules that most buyers never read. Two games with the same label can mean very different things.

This guide goes through what Valve's own documentation says about how the score is made, what it leaves out, and what to look at next to the label before you pay.

## The short version

- **Steam shows two scores:** one from the past 30 days and one for the product's lifetime. They can disagree, and the gap is useful.
- **Only purchases made on Steam count toward the score.** Reviews from activated keys, free weekends, free games and family sharing can be read, but do not all count.
- **Off-topic and review-bomb reviews are handled separately.** Some stay visible with less weight, some are removed from the score.
- **The label is a starting point.** Read a few recent reviews, and check the hours played by the reviewer.

## Two scores, not one

The Steamworks reviews page says positive and negative reviews are combined to calculate a score, and that two scores appear on the store page: "one from the past 30 days, and one for the product's lifetime." The page does not publish the exact formula.

A separate page on the store says that a recent score is listed only "if your game has released more than 45 days ago and has enough recent reviews". It does not define "enough".

What this means for you: a game with a great lifetime score and a poor recent score may have had a bad update, a server problem or a price change. A game with a poor lifetime score and a good recent score may have been fixed. Read both before you decide.

## What the score does not count

According to the same page, "Only purchases (not key activation) made by the reviewing account are included in the review score." Reviews from activated keys, free weekends, free games and family sharing still appear on the page, but "not all reviews count toward the game's review score."

If you buy keys from other sellers, our guide on [Steam keys from resellers](/guides/steam-key-reseller-risks/) covers the other risks. Here the point is smaller: a game with many key-activated players can have a score that reflects only part of its audience.

## Off-topic reviews and review bombs

Steam says off-topic reviews "will have their visibility reduced, but will still contribute to a product's review score." Moderators can mark a review as off-topic after user reports. Reviews that break Steam's rules are banned, and a banned review leaves the score.

Review bombs are treated differently. The page says "the abnormal period of volume will be removed from the overall score", and off-topic bomb reviews do not contribute to the overall score.

So a short burst of angry reviews about something other than the game may not show in the label, but it can show in the review list. Read the dates.

## Why the score matters to the store

The page also says a game at "Mixed or above (40%+)" is not penalized by its review score in algorithmic visibility, while games below 40%, in the "Mostly Negative" range, are less likely to be featured. A low score can therefore mean fewer people ever see the game, so a hidden gem with few reviews is possible. A high score does not prove it fits you either.

## Five checks before you trust a score

1. **Compare the two scores.** If recent and lifetime disagree, find out why in the newest reviews.
2. **Sort by recent.** The reviews shown most prominently are drawn from recently written ones, so look beyond them.
3. **Read one positive and one negative review in full.** Complaints about performance, bugs or price tell you more than the label.
4. **Check hours played.** A review after two hours and one after two hundred answer different questions.
5. **Match it to your PC and your play style.** Review authors may have hardware or tastes unlike yours. Check the storage and system lines too, see [what a Steam page tells you about disk space](/guides/check-game-storage-before-you-buy/).

## What we did not check

- We did not find a page from Valve that lists every label and its percentage range, other than the 40% line quoted above.
- We did not find how many reviews are needed before a score appears.
- We did not check how other stores, such as GOG or Epic, show ratings.
- The pages are written for developers. The store you see may show the score slightly differently.
