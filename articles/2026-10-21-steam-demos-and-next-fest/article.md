A demo is the cheapest way to avoid a bad purchase. You play an hour, you know whether the game is for you, and you paid nothing. Steam Next Fest, which is running right now, puts thousands of them in one place.

But demos are not all the same, and they do not always stay. This guide shows what Steam's own pages say about how demos work, when the next Next Fests are, and what to check so a demo actually helps your decision.

## The short version

- **A Steam demo is free** and is a separate item from the full game: it has its own App ID, linked to the game's App ID.
- **Steam Next Fest runs three times a year.** The current run is October 19 to 26, 2026. Only unreleased games with a playable demo can take part.
- **A demo can be switched off.** Steam lets developers deactivate it, and does not require it to stay up after the Fest. Play it while it is there.
- **A demo is a sample, not a promise.** The final game can change, and the price is not set by the demo.

## What Steam says a demo is

The Steamworks page on demos says Steam can provide free demos "so that they can try before they buy". Technically, the demo "is a separate App ID that is associated with your full game's App ID". In practice you see a link from the demo to the full game's store page, and the demo can appear in the places a free game can, such as New & Trending and tag pages.

Some limits come from the same page:

- Steam does not support matchmaking between demo players and full-game players, so do not expect to join friends who own the game.
- Valve recommends turning achievements off in demos and granting them in the full game.
- Saves can carry over only if the developer stores them in the full game's cloud storage, and only once the full game has been released. It is a suggestion, not a rule.

## When Next Fest happens

The Steamworks "Upcoming Events" page says Next Fest is "a multi-day celebration where fans can try out demos", and that it runs three times a year. The dates we read on October 21, 2026:

- October 19 to 26, 2026 (now)
- February 22 to March 1, 2027
- June 14 to 21, 2027

The page says "Upcoming (unreleased) games with a playable demo are eligible to participate." So Next Fest is a place to try games that are not out yet. It is about demos, not discounts. For sale dates, see our guide on the [Steam wishlist and sale calendar](/guides/steam-wishlist-and-sale-calendar/).

## What can disappear

The Next Fest documentation does not set an end date for demos. It says developers should "consider leaving your demo up beyond the conclusion" of the Fest, and that "it's up to you to decide what works best for your release strategy". It also asks developers to tell players if they plan to deactivate a demo.

The demos page describes what deactivation does: it "will prevent players from downloading or launching your demo", although players who already have it still see it in their library. Steam adds that a disabled demo may still run outside Steam unless it checks for a Steam license.

In plain words: if a demo looks good, install it now. If you wait for the weekend after the Fest, it may be gone, and the pages we read do not promise it will come back.

## Wishlists and demos

A wishlist is how you hear about a demo. The demos page says that after a demo first becomes playable, the developer can send email and mobile notifications to players who wishlisted the full game. That is a one-time action, available "within 14 days of the demo releasing to players for the first time". If you miss the alert, the demo is still on the store page, but there will not be a second ping.

## Five checks before you trust a demo

1. **Is it the real game?** A demo can be a slice made for the Fest. Read what the store page says it contains.
2. **Does the game have a release date?** A game with no date or no price may not be buyable for a long time. Wishlist it and move on.
3. **Do you need multiplayer?** Demo and full-game players are not matched together, so a demo says little about how online play feels.
4. **Will your progress carry over?** Do not assume it does. Treat the demo as a trial, not a head start.
5. **Does it run on your PC?** Check the minimum requirements first. A demo that runs badly tells you about your PC, and may or may not tell you about the final game.

## What we did not check

- We did not read how Steam shows demos inside your own library or which settings you have, only the developer documentation.
- We did not check other stores. GOG, Epic and others may handle demos differently.
- We cannot tell which demos will stay up after the Fest. Each developer decides.
- Dates come from a page for developers and can be moved.
