A game account holds more than games. It holds saved payment details, a Wallet balance and years of purchases. When someone else gets in, the question is not only how to get back in, but who carries the cost.

This week PC Gamer reported that Humble Bundle's redesign removed two-factor authentication. We could not read the full article or Humble's own pages, so we do not repeat details. It is a good reminder to read what a store's agreement says about account security. Here is what Steam's says, in plain words.

## The short version

- **Security is your job on paper.** Section 2.C: "You are responsible for the confidentiality of your login and password and for the security of your computer system."
- **Speed matters.** If your login may be compromised, you must notify Valve "via the support form" "without any delay."
- **Charges follow the account.** Section 3.B says you are responsible for "all charges incurred" and for "all orders or purchases made by you."
- **Refunds exist, with limits.** The refund page covers "nearly any purchase", but the usual time and play limits still apply.

## What section 2.C asks of you

Two duties sit in this section. The first is to keep your password private and your computer safe. The second is to report a possible compromise to Valve through its support form, without delay. The agreement does not define "without delay" in hours or days. In practice, the sooner you report, the less there is to untangle.

We did not find Steam Guard or two-factor authentication mentioned in the agreement. Steam's help page on Steam Guard did not show its text to our tools, so we say nothing about how it works. Check it on Steam's own site.

## Who pays for purchases

Section 3.B says you are responsible for "all charges incurred, including applicable taxes, and all orders or purchases made by you." The words are "made by you". The agreement does not say, in the text we read, what happens to purchases made by a stranger who broke into your account. That case is settled by Valve's support, and by consumer and card rules in your country. We did not review them.

What we can say from the refund page: it lets you ask for a refund on "nearly any purchase on Steam—for any reason", and Steam Wallet funds can be refunded "within fourteen days of purchase." The page does not mention hacked accounts or unauthorized purchases. So do not assume the normal refund route is the right one in a compromise. Use the support form and say clearly that the account was accessed by someone else.

## What can happen to the account

Section 4.D says "Valve is not required to provide you notice before terminating your Subscription(s) and/or Account." Section 9.C lists the circumstances in which Valve "may restrict or cancel your Account or any particular Subscription(s) at any time." The refund page adds one more consequence: "If you have been banned by VAC (the Valve Anti-Cheat system) on a game, you lose the right to refund that game." If someone cheats from your account, the consequence lands on you.

## A five-minute checklist

1. **Use a password you use nowhere else.** A leak at another site should not open Steam.
2. **Look at saved payment methods.** Remove ones you do not need.
3. **Keep the Wallet small.** Funds are easier to protect when there are few of them.
4. **Check your email for store messages you did not expect.** A surprise sign-in or purchase notice is a reason to act at once.
5. **If something looks wrong, use the support form the same day.** Section 2.C asks for it without delay.
6. **Do not share the login.** Our guide on [sharing a Steam account](/guides/sharing-a-steam-account-with-family/) covers why.
