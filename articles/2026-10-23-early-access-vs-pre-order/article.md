Two buttons on a store page can look alike: "Buy" on an Early Access game and "Pre-purchase" on a game that is not out yet. In both cases you pay now for something that is not finished. But Steam's own pages treat them as different things, and the difference decides what you can ask for if you change your mind.

This guide sticks to what Valve's pages say. It does not tell you whether any particular game is worth it.

## The short version

- **Early Access is a full purchase of a playable game.** Valve's buyer FAQ says so, and its developer rules say Early Access is not a pre-purchase.
- **A pre-purchase is a promise.** The 14-day refund clock does not start until the release date.
- **Playtime counts in both cases.** In Early Access, "any playtime will count against the two-hour refund limit."
- **A finished game is not guaranteed.** Valve's FAQ warns that "some teams will be unable to 'finish' their game."

## What Early Access means on Steam

The Early Access FAQ describes it as "immediate access to games that are being developed with the community's involvement." The game changes while you play, as players give feedback and developers add content.

Two points from the same FAQ matter for a buyer. First: "Early Access is a full purchase of a playable game." Second, the developer decides when a game is ready, and some teams will not manage to finish it. Valve's advice in the FAQ is to buy only if you enjoy the game in its current state.

Valve's Early Access rules for developers set limits that help you read a store page:

- "Early Access titles must deliver a playable game or usable software to the customer at the time of purchase."
- "Do not make specific promises about future events" and "Do not ask your customers to bet on the future of your game."
- "You may not run a permanent discount while in Early Access."
- The developer should describe the current state of the game and be specific about how long it will stay in Early Access, or explain why it cannot say.

In practice: a roadmap on a store page is a plan, not a contract. What you can judge is what runs today.

## What a pre-purchase means

Pre-purchasing pays for a game that is not released yet. Steam's refund page gives separate rules for it:

- The two-hour playtime limit still applies, "except for beta testing."
- "The 14-day period for refunds will not start until the release date."
- If the game is not playable before release, you can get a refund anytime before launch. After release, "the standard 14-day/two-hour refund period will apply starting on the game's release date."

So a pre-purchase gives you more time to decide, but only because the game is not there yet. The rule for every Steam game is the same: a refund is possible "within two weeks of purchase and with less than two hours of playtime." Our guide on [how Steam refunds work](/guides/how-steam-refunds-work/) covers the details and the cases it misses.

## Early Access refunds: the clock is short

For an Early Access game the 14 days start at purchase, and every minute you play counts. If a game needs two hours just to show whether it is stable, you may use up the limit before you can tell. Plan your first session: check the controls, performance and whether the basic loop works, and stop before the two-hour mark if you are unsure.

## Does the price change?

Valve's FAQ says "Pricing of individual games may change over time", and that some developers discount early buyers while others charge a premium. The developer rules also say the Early Access price should be "no higher than that offered on any other service or website." Because prices move, a store's "was" price is only useful with history behind it. See our guide on [real and fake discounts](/guides/real-vs-fake-game-discounts/).

## Five checks before you pay

1. **Can you play it today?** If the page shows only a trailer and no description of the current state, wait.
2. **Do you enjoy the current version?** Judge reviews from the last few weeks, not the all-time score.
3. **Does the page say how long Early Access will last?** A vague answer is allowed, but it should come with a reason.
4. **Is it a pre-purchase or a purchase?** If it is a pre-purchase, note the release date: the 14-day clock starts then.
5. **Will you stop before two hours** if the game does not convince you?

We could not open GOG's support page on early-access games (the server refused the request), so this guide says nothing about GOG or other stores. Their refund rules differ from Steam's.
