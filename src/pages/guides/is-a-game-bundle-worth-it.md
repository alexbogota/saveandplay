Three Humble bundles are running this week, and the headline numbers are loud. One bundle page says its games would cost "as much as $149 if bought full price", and the top tier costs $13. On paper that is about 91% off. But a bundle is one of the few places where the printed discount tells you almost nothing, because the number that matters is not the total. It is the price of the games you would actually play.

Here is a way to work that out in five minutes, with this week's bundles as worked examples.

In the Twisted Trilogies and Triple-i listings, a higher tier adds games on top of the lower ones. For Gals and Pals the listing gives the same kind of split, 4 + 4 + 3 games, and we count it the same way. The listings we read are from trade sites, not from Humble itself (see "What we did not check" below).

## Why "worth $149" tells you little

A bundle page adds up the list prices of every game inside. That total says what the games would cost if you bought each at its full price. It does not say what they cost in a normal week, or what they cost in the cheapest week. We covered the same trap for single games in our guide to [real and fake discounts](/guides/real-vs-fake-game-discounts/): a percentage is only as honest as the price it is measured against.

So set the big number aside and work with two smaller ones.

## The number that matters: price per game

Divide each tier's price by the number of games it gives you. Then do the same for each step up: the extra money you pay for the next tier, divided by the extra games it adds.

| | 2 | $10 | 5 | $2.00 | +$2 for 3 games ($0.67 each) |

| Twisted Trilogies | 1 | $12 | 3 | $4.00 | |
| | 2 | $15 | 6 | $2.50 | +$3 for 3 games ($1.00 each) |
| | 3 | $20 | 9 | $2.22 | +$5 for 3 games ($1.67 each) |
| Gals and Pals | 1 | $6 | 4 | $1.50 | |
| | 2 | $9 | 8 | $1.13 | +$3 for 4 games ($0.75 each) |
| | 3 | $12 | 11 | $1.09 | +$3 for 3 games ($1.00 each) |

Two things stand out.

- **The top tier always looks cheapest per game.** That is how tiers are built, so it proves nothing. What matters is whether you want the games it adds.
- **The step up can cost more than the tier below.** In Twisted Trilogies the last step costs $1.67 per game, against $1.00 for the step before it.

Now the part the table cannot show: **count only the games you would play.** If you want three of the nine games and they sit in different tiers, you pay for the highest tier you need. In Twisted Trilogies, the three Spirit Hunter games are listed only in the $20 tier. If they are the reason you are here, you pay $20 for three games you want, about $6.67 each, and the other six are a bonus you may never open.

The simple rule: **tier price divided by the games you would actually play.** If that number is more than you would pay for those games on their own, skip the bundle.

## Check the keys

- **They are Steam keys.** All three listings show Steam as the platform. A Steam game is a license on your account, which has consequences for what you can do with it later. Our guide to [Steam keys and DRM-free games](/guides/steam-key-vs-drm-free/) explains what Steam's own agreement says.
- **They expire if you do not redeem them.** Two listings say before April 30, 2027. The third gives two different dates, April 28 and April 29, which is a good reason to redeem right away and not leave keys in your inbox.
- **Subtract what you already own.** A game already in your library adds nothing, so take it out of the count before you divide.
- **Check the region.** Make sure the keys work in your country before you pay.

## A countdown is not a reason

These bundles end on October 28 and 31. A deadline is a reason to decide, not a reason to buy. Ask yourself: would I buy this game alone for $4? If the answer is no, then $1 is not a bargain, it is one more game in the backlog.

## What about the charity part?

The Triple-i listing says a portion of each purchase supports Gamers Outreach, and does not say how much. The other two listings we read do not mention charity. Because we could not read Humble's own pages, we cannot tell you how a payment is split between the publishers, a charity and Humble. If that matters to you, check it on the bundle page before you pay.

## Five questions before you buy a bundle

1. **Which games here would I actually play?**
2. **Which tier holds them, and what is the price per game I would play?**
3. **What would I pay for them alone in a normal week?** A price-history tracker shows that. Our [five questions for sale prices](/guides/real-vs-fake-game-discounts/) apply to bundles too.
4. **Do the keys fit?** Platform, region and redeem date.
5. **Would I still buy it if there were no countdown?**

## What we did not check

- **Humble's own pages.** They were not available to our tools, so every number above comes from two trade sites: gg.deals (Twisted Trilogies and Gals and Pals) and Indie Game Bundles (Triple-i). Check the bundle page before you pay, because tiers, prices and dates can change.
- **The prices of the individual games.** We did not look them up, so we do not say whether any of these bundles beats buying the games one by one. Step 3 above is how you check that yourself.
- **Gals and Pals.** The listing gives 11 games and the number of games per tier, but names only some of them in each tier. The running totals (4, 8, 11) are our addition.
