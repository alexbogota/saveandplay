Many households have one Steam account and several people using it: a partner on the couch PC, a child who "just wants to try something", a friend who borrows the login for a weekend. It works until a charge looks wrong, or until the account is locked.

Here is what Steam's own agreement says about sharing, in plain words, and a few habits that keep you out of trouble.

## The short version

- **The account is meant for one person.** Section 1.C of the Steam Subscriber Agreement says your account "is strictly personal".
- **You pay for what happens on it.** Section 3.B makes the holder responsible for purchases made by "anyone that uses your Account, including your family or friends."
- **Breaking the rules can cost the whole library.** Section 9.C says that if Valve cancels an account for a breach, no refund of "unused funds in your Steam Wallet" is granted.
- **Refunds do not care who clicked buy.** Steam's refund page covers the purchase, with its own time and play limits.

## What "personal" means

In section 1.C the agreement says you may not "reveal, share or otherwise allow others to use your password or Account" unless Valve specifically authorizes it. It also says you may not "sell or charge others for the right to use your Account, or otherwise transfer your Account."

Read this as a contract, not as a promise that every shared login is punished. We did not find any sentence in the agreement that says how Valve treats a family sharing a login in practice. We only know what you agreed to.

## Who pays when someone else buys

Section 3.B says the holder is responsible for "all orders or purchases made by you or anyone that uses your Account, including your family or friends." If a child buys a game, the agreement treats it as your purchase.

The refund page does not mention children. It says you can request a refund "for nearly any purchase on Steam—for any reason", and the rule for games is the usual one: within 14 days and under two hours of play. Our guide to [how Steam refunds work](/guides/how-steam-refunds-work/) has the details. After the window, you can still ask, but the page makes no promise.

## What can go wrong with Steam Wallet funds

Section 3.C says funds added to the Wallet are "non-refundable and non-transferable". It also says Valve may set balance limits, and that a Wallet may not exceed US$2,000 or its local equivalent. If an account is cancelled under section 9.C, the agreement says "no refund, including of any Subscription fees or of any unused funds in your Steam Wallet, will be granted." Section 4.D adds that Valve is not required to give notice before it ends an account.

So a shared login that leads to a ban affects the money in the Wallet too. Another point from the refund page: a player banned by VAC "loses the right to refund that game". If a friend cheats on your account, the consequence is yours.

## Gifts instead of logins

A gift is the clean alternative. The refund page says an unredeemed gift can be refunded within the usual 14 days and two hours, and a redeemed gift can be refunded only if the recipient starts the request, with the money going back to the person who paid. The game then sits on the recipient's own account.

## Five-minute habits

1. **Do not give out the password.** Keep your account to yourself, as section 1.C asks.
2. **Check what payment methods are saved.** Anything saved on a shared PC can be used by whoever sits there.
3. **Keep the Wallet small.** Funds are non-refundable and non-transferable, so a low balance limits the damage.
4. **Test and decide fast.** If a wrong purchase happens, request the refund early, before the 14 days and two hours run out.
5. **Give games, not logins.** A gift ends up on the right account.
