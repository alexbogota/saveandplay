Nobody checks disk space until the download stalls at 90%. Big releases make it a real question: a game that wants 145 GB can fill a small SSD on its own, and you only find out after you have paid.

The answer is on the store page, under system requirements, and it takes one minute to read. This guide shows what that line says, what it leaves out, and what we found on five real Steam pages.

## The short version

- **The storage line is the number the publisher gives for the installed game.** It is not a promise about what you need during the download or after later updates.
- **Read the notes next to it.** Phantom Blade Zero's page adds "SSD installation required."
- **Some pages have no storage line at all.** If it is missing, ask before you buy, or check the game's own site.
- **Labels differ.** "Storage", "Hard Drive" and "Hard Disk Space" all appear on the pages we read.

## What the line is, and where to find it

On a Steam store page, scroll to "System Requirements". There are Minimum and Recommended columns, and a tab for each operating system the game supports. The storage line sits in the same list as the processor and memory.

Check the tab for your own system. On the No Man's Sky page, the Minimum for Windows lists "Storage: 15 GB available space", while the Recommended columns for Windows and macOS have no storage line. The SimCity 4 Deluxe Edition page lists "Hard Disk Space: 2 GB" for macOS and "Hard Drive: 1.6GB" for Windows. Same game, two different numbers, depending on the tab.

The spread is large: the biggest figure here is about 90 times the smallest. A recent big release is not a typical case, but it is the one that surprises people.

## The notes matter as much as the number

Phantom Blade Zero's page says, under both Minimum and Recommended: "SSD installation required." So the question is not only whether you have 145 GB free, but whether that space is on a solid-state drive. A large old hard drive does not satisfy that line.

Look for other notes in the same box: required launchers or third-party agreements (Thronebreaker's page says "Requires agreement to a 3rd-party EULA"), operating-system support, and platform warnings. A requirement you cannot meet is easier to catch before purchase than after.

## What the line does not tell you

- **Space for downloading.** The page gives an "available space" figure for the game. We did not find anything on these pages about extra room during download or install, and we could not verify the claim that one game's pre-load asks for double its file size (see below).
- **Future patches.** The number describes the game as it is listed now. Updates can change it.
- **Optional parts.** Language packs, texture packs and DLC may or may not be counted.
- **Missing lines.** If a page leaves out the line, you simply do not know.

## A five-minute check

1. Open the System Requirements section and select your operating system's tab.
2. Find the storage line, whatever it is called.
3. Read the notes: SSD, launcher, extra agreements.
4. Compare with your drive's free space, and leave some margin for updates.
5. If the line is missing, look at the publisher's site or the community forums before you pay.
6. If you cannot fit the game, remember what the refund rules allow. Our guide to [how Steam refunds work](/guides/how-steam-refunds-work/) explains the 14-day, 2-hour rule.

## What we did not check

We read only the five Steam pages above. We did not install any of these games, so we cannot say whether the real download or install size matches the listed number. Valve's support pages and several news sites did not open for our tools, so we did not verify reports that Phantom Blade Zero's pre-load needs about 300 GB. We say nothing about how other stores list requirements. Pages can change after October 29, 2026.
