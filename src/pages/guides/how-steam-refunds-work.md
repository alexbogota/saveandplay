A refund policy is the cheapest insurance a game buyer has, and most people only read it after something goes wrong. A game runs badly on your PC, a demo was nicer than the real thing, or you bought the wrong edition. By then it is easy to miss that the rule has a clock on it.

Here is what Steam's own refund page says, in plain words, and the cases where the rule does not help you.

## The short version

- **The basic rule:** Steam's page says its refund offer applies "within two weeks of purchase and with less than two hours of playtime" for games and software bought on the Steam store.
- **Keys from other stores are outside it.** The page says "Valve cannot provide refunds for purchases made outside of Steam", and that includes third-party keys.
- **Missing the window is not always the end.** The page says you can ask anyway, and Steam will take a look. It also says it may stop offering refunds to people who abuse them.

## What the rule says

The core sentence on the refund page is: "The Steam refund offer, within two weeks of purchase and with less than two hours of playtime, applies to games and software applications on the Steam store." Valve says it will issue a refund "for any reason" on request. You do not have to prove that the game is broken.

If the request is approved, the page says you are refunded within a week. The money goes back as Steam Wallet funds or to the original payment method. If the original method cannot be refunded, the full amount goes to your Steam Wallet.

Two practical points follow from this:

- **The clock is two numbers, not one.** Fourteen days of ownership and two hours of play both count. A game you bought 10 days ago and played for 3 hours is outside the offer, even though you are inside the 14 days.
- **Early Access counts.** For a game in Early Access or Advance Access, the page says any playtime counts against the two-hour limit.

## Cases that work differently

The refund page has a section for each of these. The wording below is our summary of those sections.

- **Pre-purchases.** If the game cannot be played before release, you can ask for a refund at any time before release. The 14 days and two hours then start on the release date.
- **DLC.** Refundable within 14 days, with the same two-hour limit on the base game, as long as the DLC has not been consumed, modified or transferred. Some third-party DLC is marked non-refundable on the store page before you buy.
- **Bundles.** A bundle can be refunded in full if none of its items were transferred and the combined playtime of all items is under two hours. If a bundle includes non-refundable DLC or an in-game item, Steam says it will tell you at checkout whether the whole bundle is refundable.
- **In-game purchases.** In games made by Valve, 48 hours if the item is unused. For other developers' games the page says: "in-game purchases in non-Valve games are not refundable through Steam."
- **Gifts.** An unredeemed gift follows the normal rules. A redeemed gift can be refunded if the recipient asks, and the funds go back to the person who paid.
- **Steam Wallet funds.** The page allows a refund of Wallet funds within fourteen days of purchase if you have not used any of them. The Subscriber Agreement (section 3.C) says funds added to the Wallet are otherwise non-refundable.

## Where the rule does not help

**A key you bought in another store.** If you paid a key reseller, Valve's page says it cannot refund purchases made outside Steam. Your refund path is the seller's own policy, which can be very different. Check it before you buy, not after. Our guide to [Steam keys and DRM-free games](/guides/steam-key-vs-drm-free/) explains why a key is a different thing from a store purchase.

**Repeated refunds.** The page calls refunds a way "to remove the risk from purchasing titles on Steam—not as a way to get free games" and says that if it looks like abuse, "we may stop offering them to you."

**VAC bans.** According to the page, a player banned by VAC loses the right to a refund for that game.

## How to ask, and what to do first

Requests go through Steam Support at help.steampowered.com. A few habits help:

1. **Test early.** Launch the game on the day you buy it, so you find a crash or poor performance while you have an hour and a half left.
2. **Watch the playtime clock.** Check your library's played hours before you decide.
3. **Decide before day 14.** Do not leave a doubtful purchase until the last evening.
4. **If you are past the limits, ask anyway.** The page says Steam will take a look, but it makes no promise.
