There are two ways to get a server for you and your friends: run it on your own computer, or rent one from a hosting company. Both work. Which one is better depends on how often you play, how many people join, and how much setup you want to do.

## Hosting at home

You install the server software on your PC (or a spare computer) and let your friends connect to it.

**Good**

- No monthly fee for the server itself.
- Full control over the files and settings.
- A good way to learn how servers work.

**Not so good**

- The server runs only while the computer is on. If you turn it off, your friends cannot play.
- Players from outside your home network need a path in. The Minecraft wiki explains that port forwarding creates a bridge between the internet and your server on your local network, and you have to set it up on your router.
- Friends connect to your home address, so that address is visible to the people who join.
- The server uses your computer's power and your internet connection. Playing and hosting on the same PC can slow both down.

## Renting from a host

You pay a hosting company, and it runs the server in a data center. You manage it from a web control panel.

**Good**

- The server is online all the time, even when your PC is off.
- No router settings or port forwarding on your side. The host gives you an address to share.
- Hosts usually advertise extras such as protection against attacks and automatic backups. Check what is included in the plan you pick.
- You can usually choose a data center close to your players.

**Not so good**

- A monthly cost.
- You depend on the host's quality and support. We have not tested any host, so read what the provider promises with a critical eye and check the refund terms.
- A control panel can limit some advanced settings.

## Which one should you pick?

| Your situation | A good choice |
|----------------|---------------|
| Testing a game or a mod for a weekend | Home |
| A few friends who play together on certain evenings | Home, or a small rented plan |
| Friends who want to join at any hour | Rent |
| A bigger group, a large world or many mods | Rent, with enough memory |
| You do not want to touch router settings | Rent |

## A sensible way to start

Try home hosting first if you are curious and have a spare evening. If it becomes a hassle, because the PC is off when friends want to play, or the setup is too fiddly, move the same world to a rented server. Before you buy, work out how much memory you need with our guide on [how much RAM a game server needs](/servers/how-much-ram-does-a-game-server-need/), and follow the [step-by-step guide](/servers/start-a-game-server-for-friends/).
